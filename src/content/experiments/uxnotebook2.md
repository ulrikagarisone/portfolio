# Unpredictable Washroom Faucet Pressure

## Context
**Location:** Campus women's washroom  
**Situation:** Student uses sink, gets splashed by unexpectedly high water pressure, clothes get wet.

## The UX Problem
Manual washroom faucets blast water too strongly *instantly* upon being turned, even with minimal rotation, soaking users' clothing unintentionally. The **mechanical mechanism provides no meaningful gradual control** over the flow.

**Observed scenario:**
- User approaches sink to wash hands.
- User reaches for the **manual tap handle (screw-type)**.
- User *gently* turns the handle, expecting a slow, low-pressure start.
- Water **BLASTS at near-full pressure instantly** with the smallest opening.
- Water splashes violently onto the user's shirt/pants.
- User jumps back, embarrassed and frustrated, and struggles to quickly close the tap.
- Now wet for the rest of the day.

## Why It Matters
**Emotional impact:**
- Embarrassment and loss of confidence
- Anxiety about using that washroom again
- Ruined outfit affects mood for hours
- Self-consciousness in public/classes

**Practical impact:**
- Increased cleaning burden (water everywhere)
- Accessibility issue (unpredictable = scary for some users)
- Wastes water (users jump back, water keeps running)
- Potential clothing damage (delicate fabrics)

## Root Causes
1. **Faulty or uncalibrated internal water pressure** settings for the fixture.
2. **Poor plumbing fixture design** where the valve goes from 0% flow to 80%+ flow with minimal rotation. (The valve lacks a proper *taper* or *throttling* capability.)
3. **No flow restrictor** installed to limit the maximum output rate.
4. **One-size-fits-all approach** ignores diverse user needs

## Solution Directions

### Immediate Fix (Today)
- Adjust pressure regulator to lower maximum output
- Add clear warning sign: "Strong water pressure"
- Schedule regular maintenance checks
- Add splash guard around faucet area

### Better Solution (This Month)
- Replace the current faucet with one known for good throttling capability
- Install an accessible, easily adjustable in-line stop valve beneath the sink, allowing maintenance staff to fine-tune the maximum flow rate specifically for that tap.

**Visual feedback system:**
- LED indicator shows pressure level
- Blue = gentle, Green = normal, Red = strong
- Users can choose before activating

## UX Principles Applied
- **Error prevention** — Anticipate and prevent problems
- **User control and freedom** — Let users adjust to preferences
- **Consistency and standards** — Predictable behavior reduces anxiety
- **Accessibility** — Works for all users, all abilities
- **Feedback** — Gradual changes communicate system state

## Related Patterns
- Progressive disclosure
- Graceful degradation
- Universal design
- Sensory feedback systems
- Adaptive interfaces

## Real-World Examples
**Good implementations:**
- Japanese washrooms: Gentle flow sensors with clear markings
- High-end hotels: User-adjustable pressure controls
- Modern airports: Gradual ramp-up systems

## Accessibility Considerations
**Who this affects most:**
- Children (scared by sudden, powerful spray)
- Elderly users (may lose balance from surprise)
- People with sensory sensitivities (PTSD, autism spectrum)
- Wheelchair users (can't dodge easily)
- Anyone wearing formal/delicate clothing

---

**Key Takeaway:** Physical UX matters as much as digital. Small design oversights create daily frustrations. Design for the most sensitive user — it helps everyone.
