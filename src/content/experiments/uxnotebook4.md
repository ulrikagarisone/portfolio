# Nowhere to Place Bags at Airport Gate

## Context
**Location:** Charleroi Airport gate area  
**Situation:** Woman with new bag has no clean place to put it except dirty floor while waiting for boarding.

## The UX Problem
Passengers must choose between:
- Putting clean luggage on dirty floor (undesirable)
- Holding bag in lap entire wait (uncomfortable, 30-60+ min)
- Balancing bag awkwardly between feet (unstable, anxious)
- Leaving bag blocking walkway (inconsiderate, risky)

No good option exists. Design assumes bags don't exist or don't matter.

**Observed scenario:**
- Woman arrives at gate with new luggage
- Looks for hook, shelf, or ledge → none exist
- Reluctantly places bag on floor
- Looks uncomfortable, keeps checking it
- Tries pulling it onto chair → takes up seat
- Other passengers face same problem
- Floor cluttered, walking paths blocked

## Why It Matters
**Hygiene & comfort:**
- Airport floors are notoriously dirty (thousands of shoes daily)
- New bags immediately soiled
- Psychological discomfort about cleanliness
- Taking bag home = bringing airport floor into house

**Practical issues:**
- Awkward posture from holding bag
- Can't relax during wait
- Risk of theft (bag on floor, out of sight)
- Trip hazards for other passengers

**Emotional impact:**
- Frustration with poor design
- Feeling disrespected ("my belongings don't matter")
- Anxiety about bag safety
- Reduced travel satisfaction

## Root Causes
1. **Seating designed without luggage consideration**
2. **No hooks, ledges, or racks** in gate areas
3. **Cost-cutting** in airport furniture
4. **Assumption bags go overhead** (but can't until boarding)
5. **One-size-fits-all** seating ignores diverse traveler needs
6. **Short-sightedness** — didn't observe actual use patterns

## Solution Directions

### Quick Fix (This Month)
- Add adhesive hooks to seat sides/backs
- Portable luggage racks (like hotel racks) near seating
- Floor mats or "bag zones" with easy-clean surfaces
- Temporary signage encouraging use of designated areas


### Best Solution (Gate Redesign)
**Multi-function seating zones:**

Zone A: Bag-friendly seating
- Wide armrests with bag hooks
- Under-seat storage rails
- Charging ports + bag security straps

Zone B: Standard seating
- Traditional chairs for light travelers
- More density, less space

Zone C: Premium/accessible
- Extra space, tables, better bag storage
- Priority boarding nearby


**Smart luggage management:**
- Self-service bag lockers (free, 60-min)
- Digital check: "Your bag is secure in locker #12"
- Alerts when boarding starts

**Universal design principles:**
- Hooks at multiple heights (wheelchair users too)
- Visual cues showing "bag-safe" areas
- Color-coded: Green floor = clean bag zone

## UX Principles Applied
- **User context** — Design for real-world use, not ideals
- **Anticipation** — Predict user needs before they ask
- **Comfort** — Support relaxation in stressful environment
- **Dignity** — Respect user belongings
- **Inclusive design** — Works for all types of travelers


## Real-World Examples
**Good implementations:**
- Japanese airports: Hooks on every seat, luggage zones
- Singapore Changi: Bag shelves, charging stations
- Train stations: Overhead racks, under-seat storage
- Hotels: Luggage racks standard in every room

**Bad implementations:**
- Most budget airports: Zero bag consideration
- Pure-cost-optimization seating
- "Stand and wait" areas with no amenities

## Design Research Insights
**What travelers actually do:**
- 80% place bags on floor reluctantly
- 15% hold bags entire wait (causing fatigue)
- 5% find creative solutions (chair blocking, etc.)
- 100% would prefer a better option


---

**Key Takeaway:** Design for the complete user journey, not just the primary task. Bags are part of travel—ignoring them creates unnecessary stress. Good design anticipates needs before users must ask.
