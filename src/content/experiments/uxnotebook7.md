# Bike Parking: Racks Don't Fit Modern Wheel Sizes

## Context
**Location:** Public bike parking area, afternoon  
**Situation:** Modern city bike with wider tires doesn't fit into standard rack slots.

## The UX Problem
Infrastructure designed for old bike standards (narrow 23-28mm tires) excludes modern bikes:
- Electric bikes (wider frames)
- Cargo bikes (fat tires)
- Mountain bikes (chunky treads)
- Family bikes (stability features)

Users forced to lock bikes insecurely elsewhere.

**Observed scenario:**
- Girl arrives with modern bike (35mm tires)
- Tries to slide wheel into rack slot
- Wheel too wide → doesn't fit
- Tries forcing it → risks damaging wheel
- Gives up, locks to nearby pole instead
- Less secure, blocks pedestrian path
- Higher theft risk

## Why It Matters
**Security impact:**
- Proper racks = secure locking
- Improvised locking = easy theft target
- Insurance may not cover improper parking

**Usability impact:**
- Discourages cycling (if parking is hard)
- Penalizes eco-friendly transport
- Excludes modern bike technology

**Urban planning:**
- Bikes blocking sidewalks
- Pedestrian accessibility reduced
- Visual clutter

## Root Causes
1. **Infrastructure designed 10-20 years ago** for outdated bike specs
2. **No updates** as bike technology evolved
3. **Fixed slots** instead of flexible systems
4. **Bike diversity not considered** in design
5. **Procurement cycles** too slow for rapid change

## Solution Directions

### Quick Fix
- Add **universal locking bars** next to old racks
- Temporary signage: "Wide-tire bikes - use pole section"
- Paint ground guides for proper spacing

### Better Solution
**Adjustable rack systems:**
Modern Bike Rack Features:

- Variable slot widths (adjusts 25-50mm)
- Vertical locking rails (any wheel size)
- Frame hooks (no wheel insertion needed)
- Multiple locking points

## UX Principles Applied
- **Flexibility** — Adapt to changing user needs
- **Inclusivity** — Serve diverse users/equipment
- **Future-proofing** — Anticipate evolution
- **Context fit** — Match real-world use

## Related Patterns
- Modular design systems
- Adaptive infrastructure
- Universal design
- Progressive enhancement
- Inclusive design

**Key Takeaway:** Infrastructure must evolve with technology. Design for tomorrow, not yesterday. Flexible systems outlast fixed ones.
