# Airport Water Bottles: Size, Price, and Waste

## Context
**Location:** Charleroi Airport retail area, afternoon  
**Situation:** Thirsty traveler faces only two options: tiny €3 bottle (250ml) or massive €4 bottle (1.5L). Neither is right.

## The UX Problem
Pricing and sizing force travelers into lose-lose choices:
- **Option A:** Expensive small bottle (wasteful cost, not enough water)
- **Option B:** Huge bottle (awkward to carry, creates plastic waste, too much for trip)

Both options increase waste, inconvenience, and negative brand perception.

**Observed scenario:**
- Traveler enters shop needing water for 2-hour flight
- Sees tiny bottle: "€3 for this tiny thing?!"
- Sees huge bottle: "I can't carry this around..."
- Frustrated, buys giant one
- Drinks 1/3 on plane
- Leaves 2/3 in seat pocket → waste
- Or forces into already-full bag → spillage risk

## Why It Matters
**Environmental impact:**
- Massive plastic waste from oversized bottles
- Single-use mentality encouraged
- No sustainable alternatives offered
- Contributes to airport's carbon footprint

**User experience:**
- Irritates eco-conscious travelers
- Creates guilt about waste
- Awkward carrying/storing in overhead bins
- Price gouging perception damages brand trust

**Economic inefficiency:**
- Travelers buy more than needed
- Leftover water discarded at security/boarding
- Lost opportunity for sustainable branding

## Root Causes
1. **Profit optimization over user needs** — Margins on small bottles too good
2. **No "right-size" option** for average 1-3 hour journey
3. **Missing refill infrastructure** — No water fountains post-security
4. **Retail mentality** — Sell product, not solve problem
5. **Lack of sustainable options** — No refillable program
6. **Monopoly pricing** — Captive audience, inflated costs

## Solution Directions

### Quick Fix (This Month)
- Add 500ml "traveler size" option at fair price (€2)
- Install water fountains post-security (free refills)
- Clear signage: "Bring empty bottle, fill for free"

**Sustainability messaging:**
- Discount for bringing your own bottle
- Donate €0.50 per refill to ocean cleanup
- Track "Bottles saved" on digital display

### Best Solution (Long Term)
**Circular water system:**

1. Airport provides deposit-return program
2. Buy durable bottle for €5 (€3 deposit)
3. Return at any airport in network
4. Get deposit back OR exchange for fresh one
5. Airport handles cleaning/redistribution


**Free refill stations:**
- Post-security water fountains (EU requirement)
- Bottle filling stations at every gate cluster
- Cold water, room temp, and sparkling options
- Digital counter: "You just saved bottle #47,392!"

**App integration:**

Airport app shows:
- Nearest water fountain
- "Refilled 3 times today" badge
- Savings tracker: "Saved €9 and 3 bottles this trip"


## UX Principles Applied
- **User empathy** — Solve actual needs, not maximize profit alone
- **Sustainability** — Design for environment + user
- **Choice architecture** — Offer the right options
- **Social responsibility** — Lead positive change
- **Value perception** — Fair pricing builds trust


## Real-World Examples
**Good implementations:**
- Amsterdam Schiphol: Free water fountains everywhere
- San Francisco Airport: Refill stations, track bottles saved
- Some airlines: Bring empty bottle, we'll fill it

**Bad implementations:**
- Most budget airports: Water monopoly, no fountains
- Retail-first mentality over service

---

**Key Takeaway:** Good UX considers environmental and social impact. Sustainable design isn't just eco-friendly—it's user-friendly. Solve the real problem (hydration) not the business problem (maximize bottle sales).
